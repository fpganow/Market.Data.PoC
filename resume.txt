====================================================================================================
10 GbE Cboe BATS PITCH Market Data Parser on AMD Kria KR260            Jan 2025 - present (personal)
====================================================================================================
LabVIEW FPGA, Verilog/SystemVerilog, Vivado 2024.1, Vitis/PetaLinux, C/C++, Python | github.com/fpganow/Market.Data.PoC

Built a hardware pipeline that takes Cboe BATS/EDGX "PITCH" multicast market data straight off a
10 Gigabit Ethernet link and turns it into normalized order-book messages inside the FPGA fabric,
end to end on an AMD Kria KR260 (Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoC). Parsing logic authored in LabVIEW FPGA,
exported as a netlist and integrated into a hand-built Vivado block design (10G PCS/PMA, custom
XGMII-to-AXI-Stream adapters, AXI DMA, capture FIFOs); later re-implemented as a pure-RTL parser
in the same harness to find the design's speed limits.

What I did
- Designed and verified a PITCH parser handling 14 message types (Add Order long/short/expanded,
  Modify, Reduce Size, Order Executed, Order Executed at Price/Size, Delete, Trade variants, Time),
  emitting a normalized 16 x 64-bit record (type, side, order id, quantity, symbol, price,
  executed/canceled/remaining, timestamps) across a 156.25 MHz MAC domain and a 100 MHz parser loop.
- Brought the design up on real hardware: 10G SFP+ DAC from a Linux workstation (Intel E810) to the
  KR260, UDP frames sent from Python, parsed records read back over AXI on the ARM Linux side.
  Result on a 659-message, 20-frame test feed: 659/659 records, every field correct, over the
  cable and in simulation.
- Root-caused and fixed a frame-boundary corruption bug down to NI's vendor IPv4/UDP stream
  library (frames whose last 64-bit beat carries 7 or 8 valid bytes emitted one extra bogus word
  and desynchronized the parser for many frames) by writing bit-exact Python models of the vendor
  VIs and of my parser that reproduced the gate-level simulation's trace word for word; shipped a
  two-part fix (pad the final beat, clear the parser buffer at every frame start per the Cboe
  spec) and a regression pcap.
- Fixed harness bugs the hard way: an inverted 10G TX reset that had silently disabled transmit,
  a 64-bit PIO readout that popped two FIFO beats per load (replaced with 32-bit width converters),
  timing closure of the parser loop at 100 MHz, and Kria dfx-mgr overlay quirks.
- Built the test and measurement stack: a PITCH pcap generator with spec-validated message
  lengths and sequence continuity, an xsim testbench that replays pcaps through the exported
  netlist, a scorer that checks every field of every record against the pcap, a paced UDP sender
  (pps / Mbit/s / pcap-timestamp replay / top speed), a rate-sweep tool, and an in-fabric
  AXI-Stream monitor (records, sequence gaps, stall cycles, signed min/max latency, frame counts,
  a "sink" mode that measures the parser with the host readout out of the loop).
- Measured the LabVIEW design's ceiling instead of guessing: clean to 1.5 Gbit/s (6.0 M msg/s),
  desynchronizing from 2 Gbit/s, and traced the limit to the 16-word record serialization
  (160 ns per message = 6.25 M msg/s) rather than the parser state machine (6-9 cycles/message),
  then wrote the optimization plan (pack the record, one-word-per-cycle extraction, frame-level
  parallelism, no clock chasing) against Cboe's published peak rates.
- Implemented that plan as a pure-Verilog parser in the same block design (filter, one-word-in /
  one-message-out extractor, field decoder, one 1024-bit record per clock, everything in the
  156.25 MHz receive clock): 2.3k LUTs, 0 block RAM, timing closed with +0.58 ns slack.
  [Developed with an AI coding assistant; I specified, reviewed, built and measured it.]

for its busiest equities feed; the equities feeds are shaped to 1 Gbit/s.

Things I can talk about in an interview
- Why a 128-byte record at one 64-bit word per 100 MHz cycle caps you at 6.25 M messages/s, and
  how the measurement showed the knee exactly there.
- Cut-through vs store-and-forward parsing and why the parser emits the first message ~40 ns
  after its bytes land while the rest of the frame is still on the wire.
- Clock-domain crossings (toggle handshakes, async FIFOs, set_max_delay) between the 156.25 MHz
  MAC domain, the 100 MHz PL domain and the ARM side.
- Debugging an FPGA datapath you cannot probe: gate-level simulation of a vendor netlist, bit-exact
  software models, pcap-driven regression, and in-fabric counters instead of host-side capture.
- The next bottleneck: programmed-I/O FIFO readout (~8k records/s) vs a 128-bit DMA ring into
  DDR4 on an HP port (GB/s), and why the watchlist filter belongs in fabric.

field scorer, in-fabric monitor and rate-sweep tooling that made every number above measurable.
